Questions and answers

What is CommanderTestLabor?

Budget Commander decks battle each other – not at the kitchen table, but in the Forge simulator, piloted by the Forge AI. 68 decks per price class: the 64 most-played commanders on EDHREC and 4 official precons as a benchmark. They play four-player games, each deck 10 games per tournament – always against decks with a similar Elo. 143 tournaments so far, with new ones added regularly.

The goal: show which commanders win the most on a small budget – with ready-made shopping lists to build them. The site is free, ad-free and a private hobby project.

How are the rankings sorted?

What it measures: how successful a deck is under identical conditions in Forge – not which deck is best for every playgroup. Matchups, politics and the AI's limits all play a part.

By Elo rating, like in chess. Every commander starts at 1500. After every game the winner gains points and the others lose some. How many depends on the opponents: beating strong decks earns a lot, beating weak ones little – and losing to weak decks costs a lot. Placement counts too: whoever is knocked out second loses less than third or fourth. A deck that wins several games in a row climbs quickly.

Example: a 1700 deck beats 1600, 1550 and 1500 → about +6. If the 1500 deck wins instead → about +13, and the 1700 favourite loses points.

What the table shows: next to the Elo the change since the last tournament (▲/▼), the form (last 10 games: gold = win, silver = 2nd, bronze = 3rd, red = 4th) and the tier: S from 1700, A from 1600, B from 1500 (average), C from 1400, D below.

Why isn't the win rate in the table? Because on its own it misleads: decks always play against decks with a similar Elo, so strong decks face strong opponents. A deck with 49% against the top is stronger than one with 55% against the midfield. The win rate is on the deck page and shows when you hover over the Elo. At a four-player table 25% is average.

Who plays whom? Every deck plays the same number of games per tournament. Opponents come from nearby in the rankings (about ±12 places) – so #1 doesn't meet #64. Seats are assigned at random.

Why do precons play? Four official precons play unchanged in every tournament and every price class. They are a fixed benchmark: the built decks change all the time, the precons never do. That shows how much stronger a built deck is than one you buy ready-made – and how much a bigger budget helps. Precons doing worse up to €200 and €500 than up to €100 is intended: that gap shows what the extra money is worth.

The precons are not in the rankings (those show only the 64 commanders) but below them under “Benchmark”, with the rank they would have. Their games count normally: beating a precon earns little Elo, because it counts as a weaker opponent.

Games that hit Forge's time limit count neither as a win nor as a loss and are replayed up to three times. Until Nov 2, 2026 Elo was calculated from all games at once; those values are the starting point, since then it goes game by game.

Does the AI play like a human?

Not quite – that is the biggest caveat. We made the Forge AI play more like a human (see “How well does the Forge AI play?”), but it can't play 2,523 cards at all; in tournaments they are replaced by similar cards but stay on the shopping list. Another 2,100 it plays only partially well. Decks built on politics, bluffing or tricky combos tend to do worse than at a real table. The rankings are a good guide, not a final verdict.

Where do the decks come from?

From the decklists of thousands of players on EDHREC. The deck builder builds as strong a deck as possible that fits the budget and makes sure there is enough ramp, card draw, removal, board wipes and protection. After that it learns from every Forge game. 100 cards, color identity and the official banlist are always checked.

What counts towards the budget?

All 100 cards including the commander, at the Cardmarket average price (updated daily). That's why prices are in euros; in the US (TCGplayer) some cards cost noticeably more or less. The limit is hard: a deck “up to €100” costs at most €100. If prices rise later, the shopping list is rebuilt automatically.

How do I build a deck?

Every deck page has a shopping list for Cardmarket in the “Buy” tab: copy the list, create a new list under “Wants” on Cardmarket and paste it. The “1 Card name” format also works for importing into Moxfield or Archidekt.

The list is exactly the version that played in the tournament – the most successful one tested. Cards the Forge AI can't play were replaced there; the originals are listed under “Adjusted for the Forge AI” in the “Decklist” tab, where you can also copy the list with original cards.

Why is my commander missing?

The 64 most-played commanders on EDHREC are tested, so every tournament has enough strong and well-known opponents. If EDHREC's list changes, a new commander moves up; those who drop out are listed under the rankings as “No longer in the field”. If yours is missing, you can have your own list tested or request a deck (both currently for supporters only).

How strong are the decks?

Up to €100: at most 3 Game Changers (Bracket 3). Game Changers are especially powerful cards from the official Wizards of the Coast list. Combos are allowed; the official banlist applies. Other bracket guidelines (such as no early two-card combos) are not checked by the deck builder.

What do tier and verdict mean?

Tier: by Elo – S from 1700, A from 1600, B from 1500 (start and average), C from 1400, D below. Every deck starts at 1500 and gains or loses points depending on its place and the strength of its opponents.

Verdict (large at the top of every deck page): the tier in words – S Top tier, A Strong, B Solid midfield, C Below average, D Weak. Below it, one sentence says how often the deck wins; the average at a four-player table is 25%. The win-rate bar also shows the range in which the true win rate lies with 90% confidence.

Where do strengths and weaknesses come from?

From Forge's game logs. Every game is read: how much damage each card dealt, who eliminated whom, who blocked what, lands, mulligans and spells cast. Each deck is then compared with all decks in the same price class.

It is also checked which opponents a deck struggles against: many flyers, counterspells, board wipes, removal, graveyard hate or artifact/enchantment removal. Only a very clear difference (99% confidence) is shown. If there is no certain weakness, the clearest trend is shown – explicitly marked as “not yet certain”.

Logged: recorded like that in the log. Indication: a recurring pattern, but no proof – a card that only comes down in games already won looks good too.

The card lists in the “Games” tab of each deck page: Damage per game (across all games), Damage when used (only games in which the card hit), Top finishers (eliminates opponents most often), Best interaction (destroys, exiles or counters the most), Biggest walls (opposing cards that block most often), Rarely cast (candidates to cut) and Wins by turn.

Why do the decks change?

The program learns from every game. In each tournament every deck tries out one card. If it wins more often, the card stays and shows up more often in the future; otherwise it is dropped. The same swap is only retried after three weeks. The shopping list only includes a test once it has proven itself.

Can I request a deck?

Yes – because of the many requests, currently for supporters on Ko-fi only, otherwise we can't keep up with testing. Once things calm down, it will be free again. Name a commander and a budget, for example Kaalia up to €250. Then this happens:

  1. Build: exactly like the tournament decks – from the EDHREC lists, with Cardmarket prices, never above your budget, with at most 3 Game Changers for limited budgets.
  2. Test: the finished deck plays 120 games in Forge against decks of all tiers (S to D) in the same budget – with the same AI as in the tournaments. That gives win rate, placements 1–4, Elo and tier. If a swap could improve the deck, it plays the same tables again with it – we only list the swap if the deck does noticeably better.
  3. Deliver: verdict, win rate, decklist and shopping list arrive by email, plus a link to the deck page with all charts. The deck only appears publicly on this site if you agree – otherwise it is a private link, not linked anywhere and not in search engines.

Requested decks don't play in the rankings, but their page shows roughly where they would land there.

Can I have my own deck tested?

Yes – because of the many requests, currently for supporters on Ko-fi only, otherwise we can't keep up with testing (up to 3 decks per week). Once things calm down, it will be free again. Here's how it works:

  1. Submit: in the “Test your own deck” form enter your commander, decklist and email – as a link to a public Moxfield or Archidekt deck or as text (one card per line, for example exported from Moxfield, Archidekt or Cardmarket).
  2. Check: 100 cards, color identity and the banlist are checked, the price comes from Cardmarket. If something is wrong, you get an email listing the problems.
  3. Measure (a few hours; tests run around the clock, one after another in the order they arrive): your deck plays 120 games in Forge against 20 commanders from the rankings – 4 from each tier from S to D – each with a deck in the same budget as yours. That is about as many games as a deck plays in 12 tournaments, so luck doesn't decide the result.
  4. Re-test swaps (only if there is something to swap, about 1–2 hours more): if the analysis finds cards that rarely come into play and a better-fitting card with the same role within budget, your deck plays the same 120 tables again with these swaps – same opponents, same seats. We list at most 3 swaps, and only those that make your deck noticeably better (at least 0.3 places better on average, not by chance). We never swap combo pieces or cards many players of this commander choose on purpose. If no swap brings a real improvement, we don't suggest one.
  5. Results by email: verdict with tier and Elo, win rate with a confidence range, placements 1–4 and roughly where it would rank. Plus concrete tips backed by the games, up to 3 re-tested swap suggestions within budget, your result against each tier and the games in numbers: when your commander came down, which cards dealt damage and finished games, who knocked you out and which cards rarely came into play.

Your deck does not appear in the rankings. You get the results by email, plus a private link to a page with charts – not linked anywhere, not in search engines. Cards the AI can't play are replaced by similar ones in testing – the results say which.

How well does the Forge AI play – and what does that mean for the rankings?

All games are played by Forge's computer opponent. Out of the box it plays decently, but not like a human: for example it rarely attacked the player with the most life and ignored win-the-game cards like Felidar Sovereign. So at the end of October 2026 we made it play more like a human – tested in hundreds of comparison games:

  • Whoever leads gets attacked. The player with the most life – especially decks that win through life – is put under pressure.
  • Threats are taken seriously. Win-the-game cards (Felidar Sovereign & co.) and infinite combos are removed or countered as soon as they are on the battlefield. If a player is about to win, everyone attacks them – but keeps a blocker back.
  • Mulligans and board wipes like at the table: hands with no or too many lands go back, board wipes come when they hit the whole table, not just one opponent.

Combo and engine decks (e.g. Kinnan, Kenrith) are still played worse by the AI than by a human. Games are played out (up to 30 minutes per game). The rankings honestly show how strong a deck is in Forge – at a real table it can look different.
